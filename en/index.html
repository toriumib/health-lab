ONE CHANGE AT A TIME

Small habit experiments

Choose one change and record it under consistent conditions. Before-and-after differences are clues, not proof of an effect.

Carefully explore the basics

Finish work before bedtime, break up prolonged sitting, or make meal preparation easier. Start with practical changes you can sustain.

How to read comparisons

Compare the 7 days before the start with the experiment period. Missing days are excluded, and the number of recorded days is shown. Seasons, illness, work and device changes can also affect values.

Do not use this tool to experiment with medication or supplement doses, or extreme dietary restriction. Discuss those changes with a qualified professional.

LOCAL TOOLS / OPEN SOURCE

Work with health data on your Mac

Apple Health → daily CSV

In the iPhone Health app, choose “Export All Health Data.” The Mac converter summarizes sleep, steps, exercise, resting heart rate, weight and HRV into a CSV you can import in Data.

This is a manual import. The XML is processed on your Mac without sending it to an external service.

Put your daily rhythm in Calendar

Create an ICS file for a chosen date range with wake-up, activity and end-of-work events. Import it manually into Apple Calendar or another calendar. The events remain there while your Mac sleeps.

Bryan Johnson: Protocol

There is no established method for immortality. An app cannot determine whether someone is “the healthiest” or has attained enlightenment. Ken-go helps you make space for rest, activity, awareness and kindness. Spiritual exploration and medical health are distinct questions.

Start with everyday life

Care for your sleep rhythm, manageable activity, meals and relationships. WHO guidance for adults includes 150–300 minutes of moderate activity per week, but this app does not impose a universal target. Adapt activity to your health and any medical conditions.

Meditation as a brief, optional practice

Meditation may help with stress, but effects vary and unpleasant experiences have been reported. It is not a substitute for treatment; stop if it becomes distressing. We do not recommend fasting, sleep restriction, hyperventilation or prolonged intensive practice.

When you need support

If severe distress persists, contact a health professional or someone you trust. If your immediate safety is at risk, call local emergency services. In Japan, call 119 for an ambulance or 110 for police.

Use it on Android

Understanding your numbers

HRV and heart rate vary with devices, measurement times and your condition. Use them to observe your own records under consistent conditions. Discuss concerning symptoms or readings with a health professional.
